# Director's preflight: prevent avoidable image and video failures

Run this review at every active stage, including when the user changes an answer, supplies a reference, approves a still or reports a defect. Apply the relevant checks as a director (viewer experience and blocking), content creator (message and authenticity), and editor (coverage, timing, sound and continuity). Review inputs before writing dependent prompts and review the assembled tool request before submission. This module is shared by actual production and the tutorial; it is not another questionnaire to fill.

Be thorough internally and concise with the user. Do not print every checklist, reread every module on every turn, or invent defects. Surface omissions, contradictions and uncertainties that could materially affect the requested output or cause an avoidable rerun. Preparation reduces risk; it cannot guarantee a generative model will obey every constraint. An attractive image, detailed prompt, stronger model or user approval does not prove untested motion or sound.

## 1. Turn each answer into explicit production facts

For the active step, extract what the user actually specified and reconcile it with earlier accepted decisions and supplied assets. Record origin and status: explicit instruction, verified reference, accepted choice, proposed default or unknown. Distinguish a request to change the project from an example, question or hypothetical. Never silently promote a recommendation or an unanswered menu into agreement.

Check ambiguous pronouns, unnamed people/products, relative directions, undefined timing, conflicting quantities, vague camera terms and claims of exactness without evidence. Resolve contradictions from existing context first. A new explicit correction supersedes the older decision only where it applies; identify which references, prompts, shots, costs or reviews are now stale. Do not ask the user to repeat information already established.

Inspect the actual reference when possible: a filename, thumbnail or user's label is not sufficient to establish its contents, orientation or suitability. Distinguish material that is authoritative for identity, geography, product geometry, action, lighting, sound or style. Where references disagree, explain the consequence before choosing one. Keep user-controlled factual details separate from generated interpretations of unseen areas.

## 2. Flag the risk with a usable recommendation

Use this compact format for each consequential issue:

> **[Clarify before shot / Verify / Suggestion] — [specific missing or conflicting detail].**
> This may cause [concrete visible/audible defect or rerun].
> **Recommendation:** [specific value or corrective artifact and why].
> **Needed next:** [one answer, exact reference or check]; affects [asset/shot/stage].

Examples:

- **Clarify before shot — “turn left” has no reference frame.** This may put her face and the market stalls on the opposite side from your intended view. **Recommendation:** define whether it is her left or screen-left, then lock the body turn and camera turn separately. Show the proposed start/end orientation or a small arrow sketch if it resolves the ambiguity.
- **Verify — only a forward street photo is available for the selfie turn.** The reverse view may invent or mirror storefronts. **Recommendation:** acquire reverse-direction coverage or use a verified 3D camera route; the forward still can still be prepared now.
- **Clarify before video — the line and camera turn exceed the proposed timing.** This may produce rushed speech, a skipped turn or a cut. **Recommendation:** shorten the line or increase duration after timing a voice sample; show the timing conflict rather than claiming an exact fit from word count.
- **Verify — the named product's operation is not established by the reference.** This may produce an impossible button press, lens flip or grip. **Recommendation:** confirm the exact model and obtain a clear operating clip before animating that action.

Ask focused questions, usually one to three at once. Offer a recommended answer and meaningful alternatives; use sketches, beat tables or annotated stills when clearer than more prose. Request specific missing media, not a vague “more references.” A user may delegate reversible defaults. Record the choice and its consequence; do not keep asking after it is resolved.

## 3. Apply the right action boundary

| Status | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Clarify before dependent action | An unresolved creative ambiguity or contradiction can change the intended result | Show the consequence and recommendation; obtain the missing choice before that image/shot is submitted. Continue independent work |
| Verify before dependent action | A required factual/capability/reference/cost claim lacks evidence | Inspect or obtain evidence; flag the exact missing item if user input is needed. A guess or approval is not verification |
| Suggestion | A reversible choice has a reasonable default without changing intent | State and record the default, proceed within authorization; invite correction without a new permission gate |
| Ready for this stage | Relevant required inputs are resolved and the operation is feasible | Proceed within existing authorization; retain a concrete output-review plan |
| Diagnostic only | An explicitly scoped, bounded test is needed to resolve a technical uncertainty | Name the question, adequate inputs, cost cap and observable pass/fail criteria. Do not present its output as an accepted final |

Do not block a concept sketch because final export settings are not yet due. Do not submit an accuracy-sensitive final merely because the user said “approve” while a required viewpoint, reference, quote or review capability remains unresolved. If the user explicitly relaxes a creative requirement, record the revised requirement and propagate it; do not change factual claims or certify quality by approval. Missing spending authorization blocks the paid action, not the preparation.

## 4. Review by stage

| Active stage | Check the inputs now | Common rerun avoided / smallest useful remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Initial prompt / guideline | Goal, viewer, deliverable, factual fidelity, camera ownership, source assets, tool constraints, budget and unresolved choices | A polished result with the wrong viewing experience; reconstruct a compact brief and show the consequential gaps |
| Script / beats / audio | Required actions and line content fit duration; speaker, language, name pronunciation, speech pauses, music/ambience intent | Rushed speech, missing action, wrong voice or unplanned music; time a sample and revise the beats |
| Character / product / location references | Identity views, costume asymmetry, exact product, authoritative geography, front/reverse coverage and usable media inputs | Face/outfit drift or invented mechanism/layout; prepare missing views before dependent motion |
| Hero still / composition | Framing, subject orientation, camera side/height, visible hands/props, exposure, perspective and landmark anchors | An attractive still that cannot lead into the intended action; review a composition with explicit orientation |
| Storyboard / camera preview | Start, intermediate and end states; movement feasibility, occlusion, line of action, turn timing and geography | Wrong-way walking, observer-camera substitution, background reset; inspect a rough movement preview |
| Tool-ready request / generation | Prompt, actual selected reference files and roles, supported settings, duration/count/audio, exclusions, three credit options and selected charge | Correct prose with the wrong attached image, unsupported control, unintended audio or wrong billing; audit the final request itself |
| Edit / sound / finishing | Necessary coverage/handles, source frame rates, orientation, transitions, audio continuity, exact overlays and licensed sources when applicable | Uneditable shots, accidental mirrors, speed changes, masking dialogue; test the rough edit and mix |
| Final review / delivery | Actual full playback and listening, fidelity criteria, target export and asset versions | Claiming a still/transcript proves motion/pronunciation; inspect the delivered file and log remaining limitations |

## 5. Orientation, viewpoint and movement checks

For directional or turning shots, make a small **direction record**. Populate relevant fields; do not demand compass bearings for a shot that does not need them.

- **Camera source:** which physical or virtual lens records the delivered image; its operator, mounting/hand position and whether another recording source is allowed. “She films herself” is not enough to distinguish her footage from a camera watching her film.
- **Coordinates:** distinguish world/location direction, subject-relative left/right, camera-relative left/right and screen-left/right. Identify which reference is mirrored, cropped or rotated. A selfie preview may be mirrored while recorded output is not; verify the intended output rather than assuming. Do not mirror text or a product to fix orientation.
- **Start pose:** body/shoulder/hip facing, head facing, eye target, walking direction, stance and which hand holds each object. Body, face, gaze and travel can point differently; specify them independently when consequential.
- **Camera pose and move:** lens target, location, height/distance, viewing angle, movement direction, rotation direction and end state. Distinguish turning the camera from turning the person, moving the gimbal from rotating the whole grip, and a lens turn from a cut to another camera.
- **Image composition:** intended side/profile, screen position, entry/exit, lead room, headroom and crop. Verify that “moves left” describes screen travel or world travel as intended; camera motion can reverse apparent screen movement.
- **Timing and geography:** location at each beat, fixed landmarks on both sides, what should appear behind the subject on the reverse view, and continued crowd/subject travel during the turn. A 180-degree camera turn must not teleport the operator, reverse the walk or reuse the forward background as its reverse view.
- **Edit continuity:** camera side of the line of action, eyeline match, screen direction between shots and whether a deliberate crossing is motivated. An intentional continuity break is allowed when requested; identify it explicitly instead of treating all axis changes as errors.
- **Optical plausibility:** whether the recording device can appear in its own footage, considering actual lens position, reflections and grip. Do not require a full device beauty shot from its own lens or add a floating observer to satisfy “device visible.” Resolve incompatible requirements before generation.

Represent each important beat as `time | camera source/target/move | body/head/gaze | travel direction | hand/prop state | visible landmarks | dialogue`. Do not assume start/end frames alone specify a plausible continuous path. For an image, review the static state; for video, review transitions and intermediate states too.

## 6. Character, action, product and environment checks

### Character and physical action

- Same adult identity, face proportions, hairline, hair style, body proportions, age presentation and costume across relevant references; this user's character sheets include front, both profiles and back plus needed three-quarter/face views. A turnaround board is not proof of a working 3D model or accurate unseen anatomy.
- Handedness, which shoulder carries a bag, jewelry, logo placement, sleeve length, pockets, buttons and other asymmetric details; keep these consistent through turns rather than mirroring them.
- Pose and action feasibility: balance, stride, foot-ground contact, joint range, grip, contact pressure, reach, fingers, gaze and mouth movement. Account for occupied hands; someone holding a camera and food cannot simultaneously perform a two-handed action without a planned transfer.
- Start-to-end object states, entrances/exits and physical interactions: objects remain attached until released, occluded objects reappear consistently, clothing follows motion, and people do not pass through stalls or one another.
- Performance matches intent and recording source: speaking to the lens versus looking at the street, brief eye contact, natural walk and breath, believable expression and reaction timing.

### Products and props

- Verify exact product name/model/version against authoritative evidence when accuracy matters. Do not invent availability, dimensions, controls or a mechanism from a plausible product name. Resolve conflicting user names and reference images.
- Match silhouette, lens/screen position, buttons, branding, materials, size relative to the hand and actual supported operating states. Determine what details must be legible in the planned crop.
- Verify the demonstrated sequence, dominant hand, grip changes, attachment/mount and button accessibility. Establish whether the lens or body rotates and whether the visible screen should change.
- Route exact labels, screens or brand text through controlled assets/compositing when needed. Do not present generated nonsense as accurate Korean writing, UI or product branding.

### Location, lighting and continuity

- Distinguish a location-inspired scene from a factually accurate street. Use real photos/walking footage with relevant forward, reverse and side coverage; record capture viewpoint and meaningful date/season differences when they affect fidelity.
- Preserve accepted street layout and stall positions. Identify conflicts between saved scene geometry and real references instead of silently rebuilding either. Photos do not establish unseen geometry or automatic camera calibration.
- Lock important signs, storefronts, street widths, stall spacing, curb/ground pattern, crowd scale and recurring people/vehicles. Specify which elements may move and which must remain fixed.
- Check occlusion/reveal geometry, believable parallax, reflections, shadows, light direction, exposure, white balance, materials and depth of field. Night scenes need coherent practical light sources and retained highlight/shadow detail, not brightness or bloom alone.
- For cooking, check steam origin and direction, heat-light behavior where visible, food handling and continuity of utensils, portions and vendor interactions. Use the detail the shot can actually reveal.
- Inspect the actual saved Blender scene when used: relevant asset versions, coordinate axes/scale, active camera, lens/framing, frame range, camera constraints/path, rigs, occlusions, visible collections, materials/lights and render output. A still reference does not transfer 3D control to a generator.

## 7. Sound, timing, edit and technical checks

- Exact dialogue, speaker, language and intended delivery; critical names have native spelling and reliable audio evidence. Check pronunciation by listening, not romanization or ASR alone. Mark estimates until a timed sample exists.
- Speech duration plus breath/pauses and camera/hand actions fit the shot. Visible speaking requires lip-sync review; narration should not accidentally animate the visible character's mouth. Establish off-screen voice and sound perspective for POV turns.
- Music/no-music choice, source and usage rights when applicable, cue starts/ends, ambience, room/street tone and effect timing. Avoid double music/audio from both generation and editing; preserve intelligibility and inspect the final mix through listening.
- Confirm exact input roles, count/size limits and supported audio/control features in the tool's current schema. Review first/last-frame order, selected identity image, orientation, crop, mask and reference version. Negative wording is not a substitute for feasible positive instructions and required control inputs.
- Match runtime, source versus delivery resolution, aspect ratio, frame rate, audio version, subtitles/text, safe framing and export destination. Include trim handles when needed and quote the actual billed duration/count. Do not change speed or crop away required action merely to fit delivery.
- In Resolve/After Effects handoffs, check source paths/versions, timeline timebase, color management, transforms, flips, stabilization, retiming, tracking, overlays, audio routing and source relinking as applicable. A generic “fix in post” is not a tested repair route.
- Check whether tools can actually inspect the required details: frame decoding and transcription are helpful but do not establish full motion, lip-sync or pronunciation. Arrange a capable reviewer for unresolved perception needs before relying on the test.

## 8. Final prompt audit and verification record

Immediately before a dependent image/video submission, compare the actual request against the resolved brief, selected references and direction record. Check for conflicting old instructions, wrong reference order, unsupported settings, missing critical facts and accidental substitutions. In particular, reject prompt conflicts such as own-camera footage plus an unrequested tracking observer; forward travel plus an unintended reverse walk; fixed layout plus incompatible backgrounds; or exact text plus no workable text-control route.

Summarize only material readiness: `Ready for [specific action]` or `Needs [specific resolution] before [affected action]`, together with the generation-credit comparison and selected charge. Do not require another approval when the user's choices and spending authorization already cover the resolved action.

Keep a compact record in [project state](../assets/project-state.md): `stage/shot | missing/conflicting detail | possible defect | recommendation | severity/action boundary | answer or evidence | status | dependent assets`. For resolved direction-sensitive shots, save the direction record with the shot. When input changes, invalidate affected prompts, quotes and checks and review only those dependencies.

After output, compare the intended and observed start/middle/end states and review full motion/sound using [production readiness](production-readiness.md). Wrong direction, wrong camera, unstable landmarks, hand/prop errors or failed speech checks require a targeted correction or a visibly recorded limitation. Do not mark them passed because a different feature looks good. Explain whether the smallest reliable remedy is prompt/reference correction, a controlled render, selective edit, new footage or a bounded rerun; show revised credits before another paid attempt.
