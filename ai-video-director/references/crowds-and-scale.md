# Crowds, interaction groups and scale

Load for population variety, multiple interacting groups or a scale reveal. Do not build a cast bible for every distant extra.

## Identity versus population design

Keep recurring heroes on their accepted identity references. A population reference instead defines shared design rules and allowed variation: silhouettes, faces, hair, costume, proportions and motion. Use a few representative variants when useful. Label a board as either different people or multiple views of one person; confusing those roles can clone a hero throughout the crowd.

Match variation to viewing size. Distant silhouettes need readable spacing and movement more than distinct facial pores. Promote a background person to an identity record only when repeat appearances or close interaction require it. Avoid contradictory face references in shots where only population styling should transfer.

## Assign relationships and space

For consequential interactions, record `group/person | partner or target | action | world path | screen direction | exclusions | start/end state`. In a contest between teams, specify the intended opposing pairs; shared clothing alone does not define who interacts with whom. Check collisions, gaze/contact, prop ownership, occupied space and occlusion. Use [spatial blocking](spatial-blocking.md) for complex paths.

When many agents, vehicles and camera moves compete, compare simpler blocking or tighter coverage that preserves the intended event. Do not automatically remove a required wide shot; controlled layers, real footage or 3D may better preserve its geography. More random takes are not a substitute for deciding which relationships must hold.

## Fix relative scale

Use stable nearby anchors and stated proportions: a small figure relative to a grass blade, boot or doorway. Record which objects share the same world scale. A camera descending, zooming or changing focus changes perspective and attention, not the actual size of the cast or set. Keep anchor relationships through intermediate movement and occlusion; endpoint stills alone can conceal resizing.

Accept sufficient population variety, correct membership and partners, stable hero identity, plausible occupancy and persistent relative scale at delivery size. Review full motion. If the world changes size or participants merge/swap, inspect role conflicts and blocking before choosing a bounded repair or rerun.
