# Worked example: a 15-second educational Short

Fictional planning example, not a generated or audience-tested video. Timings are proposed and must be checked in an animatic. This example demonstrates Quick mode without a character sheet or paid-generation requirement.

## Brief

- Purpose: help new filmmakers distinguish camera movement from zoom.
- Audience/platform: English-speaking beginners watching a vertical social video.
- Runtime/delivery: 15 seconds, 1080 x 1920, 30 fps proposed for review; verify destination before final export.
- Tools: GPT reasoning, Blender and Resolve Free only.
- Style: clean tabletop demonstration, neutral background, two simple cubes at different depths.
- Budget: no paid generation authorized.
- Deliverable now: production plan and editable-title copy.

## Route and requirements

Use a Blender blockout because a controlled camera can demonstrate the difference without relying on generated geometry. Resolve handles the edit and exact labels. No Higgsfield, face board, voice casting or trend research is needed. Required for render: cube positions, camera states, timeline and output settings. These can be established as reversible scene defaults.

## Proposed sequence

| Shot | Time | Image/action | Exact overlay | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S01 | 0-3 s | Two cubes at different depths; camera stationary | "Zoom or move?" | Short opening cue, optional |
| S02 | 3-7 s | Camera position fixed; focal length changes | "ZOOM: camera stays put" | Optional restrained musical bed |
| S03 | 7-11 s | Focal length fixed; camera moves forward | "DOLLY: camera moves" | Same bed |
| S04 | 11-15 s | Side-by-side freeze/comparison with matched foreground size | "Notice the background size." | Gentle finish |

Total 15 seconds / 450 frames at 30 fps. Captions are not needed for absent speech; these overlays are explanatory graphics. Test reading time on a phone-size preview.

## Production instruction

> Create one simple tabletop scene with a small foreground cube and a larger cube farther back. Keep objects and lights fixed. Render two camera variants: fixed position with changing focal length, and fixed focal length with forward movement. Arrange the end frames so the foreground cube is approximately the same screen size in each comparison. Preserve the background object and scene dimensions. Use editable titles in finishing, not baked text in the scene render.

Inspect the framing rather than guessing lens values. Use the current Blender camera API/settings. If the background difference is too subtle, adjust distances in the blockout. This is an educational demonstration, so its geometry must actually support the explanation.

## Savings and acceptance

Reuse one scene and lighting setup. Test viewport previews before final rendering. No AI-video credits are required by this route, but local render/editing time still has a cost. Use a lower-resolution motion preview to evaluate the concept, then render final frames after the comparison reads clearly.

Pass if the subject-size comparison is fair, the camera modes are actually different, the labels are exact and readable, and the exported file meets the agreed specs. If no render is executed, report the plan/script as prepared and the visual demonstration as unverified.
