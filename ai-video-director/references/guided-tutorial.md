# Guided tutorial: make a video one decision at a time

## Tutor contract

Start this lesson through **`$ai-video-director-tutorial`**. Use **`$ai-video-director`** for actual project work without lesson pacing. This file is shared curriculum loaded by the companion tutorial skill; it does not make every director invocation a lesson.

Use this mode when the user asks to learn, run a tutorial or work through the process step by step. It is an interactive conversation led by the assistant, not a long article, software manual or form the user must fill. Use the user's project if supplied. Otherwise offer a small practice project without assuming they want a film career, paid tools or advanced 3D.

Begin with the Director's Project Guideline from [intake](intake.md), then Step 1 in the same response. Show a short roadmap once; expand only the active step. Record choices, examples, outputs, outstanding evidence and the next action in [project state](../assets/project-state.md). A resumed tutorial continues where it left off.

Include the shared [model/effort and conversation recommendation](model-and-context.md) in that opening overview. Explain the choice in one plain-language sentence: simple lesson choices need less reasoning than solving difficult continuity problems. Name a verified available model and supported effort; distinguish recommended from actually active. Explain cache uncertainty without an economics lecture or another mandatory learner question. At meaningful stage changes, briefly update the recommendation if needed. A fresh task is a proposal until the user explicitly requests it; save the lesson's decisions and pending choice first.

When a step proposes image or video generation, show the shared [three-option credit comparison](efficiency.md#three-generation-credit-options): lower credits, balanced and higher credits, their costs/differences and your recommendation. Explain why the recommended route fits the learner's project. Show image and video totals separately, keep hypothetical teaching prices clearly labeled, and do not generate to discover a price. Reuse known preferences and sufficient authorization; do not add an approval-only lesson turn for every repeated batch.

Apply [director preflight](director-preflight.md) to the learner's current answers and actual references. Explain consequential missing details in plain language: “This is missing and may affect your image/video by [specific defect]. I recommend [specific fix].” Ask the focused clarification before the dependent action; reuse previous answers and continue independent preparation. Teach the relevant distinction, such as her left versus screen-left or turning her head versus turning the recording lens, with a small example or preview when helpful. Do not expose the whole checklist at once.

At each step:

1. Say **Step N — [plain-language goal]** and explain what the user will accomplish in one or two sentences.
2. Explain the relevant component only when needed: what it does, why it helps this project and what effort or limitation it adds. Define an unfamiliar term once.
3. Ask one focused question with two or three meaningful choices, recommending one with a reason. Accept free text and reuse answers already supplied. When a choice is already settled, do the work rather than ask it again.
4. After the answer, write the actual prompt/plan or perform the available authorized action. Present a small concrete result and explain what to look for. Do not make the learner author technical prompts unaided.
5. Check the result against the step's purpose, record the decision and advance to the next needed choice. If a required input is missing, continue independent preparation and leave the dependent work pending.

Do not dump all the questions below at once or march through irrelevant modules. Do not require a separate “approve” after every answer. A substantive choice is enough to proceed within scope. Wait for the learner's answer when the exercise depends on their choice; no response is not a selection. If they say “recommend” or “you choose,” choose and continue with an explanation.

Support these plain-language controls: **explain more**, **show an example**, **recommend**, **change my answer**, **go back**, **pause**, **resume**, **skip optional work**, and **switch to production mode**. Going back invalidates only dependent decisions/assets. Pausing ends the lesson with a compact resume note; it does not create an automation.

Distinguish **practice/planning** from **making a real deliverable**. A beginner can complete a useful lesson with a brief, shot plan, reference checklist, written prompts and edit plan without generating media. Illustrative samples are labeled examples; placeholder previews are not rendered footage. Real execution uses available tools, verified inputs, review capability and existing sufficient authorization. Never introduce a subscription, purchase, paid generation or publication merely to complete a lesson.

## Opening example: new learner, no project yet

Use this as a pattern, not fixed wording or a default project imposed on the learner:

> **Director's Project Guideline**
>
> We'll turn one small idea into a clear video plan, then make it if you want and the tools are available. I'll explain each decision and write the prompts. We'll use references, check picture and sound, and choose software only when it helps. We can start with a planning exercise without paid generation.
>
> Our path is: 1 choose the idea and references; 2 write the short story and sound plan; 3 prepare the look; 4 preview movement; 5 make the selected shots; 6 edit and review. Unknown choices remain open, and we can skip irrelevant steps.
>
> Model/effort: [available lightweight model / supported low effort] for these first simple choices; [active setting status]. We'll continue here to retain your answers. [Cost unit]; cache savings are unverified. I'll reassess when the work becomes more complex. Replace these brackets using current evidence.
>
> **Step 1 — Choose a small project**
>
> Which starting point would you like?
> - **Your own idea** — recommended if you already have something in mind.
> - **Edit a few phone clips** — a useful introduction to editing and sound.
> - **Plan a short fictional travel scene** — useful for learning references and camera choices without filming.
>
> You can also describe a different project.

If the user already provided the idea, use it and ask the next actual gap instead. Do not ask them to choose a practice idea, repeat their duration or resupply files already available.

For a learner with phone clips, shorten the roadmap to **review clips and viewing format -> arrange story/sound -> rough edit -> review/export**. Explain that authentic recorded people do not require generated character sheets and usable footage does not require rebuilding the location in Blender. Begin with the missing viewing destination or clip access, then introduce the editor when needed.

## Step 1 — Brief and reference check

**Teach:** the brief describes the viewer's experience, not just the objects in the frame. A woman holding a camera and footage recorded by that camera are different shots.

**Guide:** extract purpose, audience, duration, aspect, viewpoint, must-preserve details and existing assets. Resolve only the gaps that change preparation. After the idea is known, establish planning practice versus a real deliverable and tools/access as needed; existing answers take priority over new menus.

**Choices when relevant:** own-camera POV / observer camera / mixed edited coverage. Or existing footage / new imagery / a combination. Use only the question needed now, not every category.

**References:** inventory what exists. Recommend real location photos for accurate architecture/signs and walking footage for geography/motion; multi-angle identity for a recurring person; exact product photos/dimensions and mechanism evidence for a prop action; source audio for a name or voice requirement. Explain the exact missing view or detail and why it matters. Research suitable public facts; ask the user for their own specific images/videos/prop references when needed. Follow [visual development](visual-development.md#reference-acquisition-before-dependent-prompts).

**Small result:** a few-line brief and a reference list marked available, to acquire, not needed or unresolved. Explain which missing item affects the next step. Do not generate an accurate-location hero while its factual references remain absent.

## Step 2 — Story, dialogue and background sound

**Teach:** short videos need enough time for actions, camera turns, words and pauses. Sound can carry information without adding another shot.

**Guide:** draft a beginning, main action and ending using [story](story.md). Offer spoken dialogue, voiceover or no speech only when unresolved. Draft the words yourself, preserve mandatory copy, and time an actual sample before dependent lip-sync work. For foreign/place names, follow the reliable pronunciation and listening checks in [audio](audio-captions.md).

**Music choice when unresolved:** natural ambience only / quiet instrumental bed / music-led edit. Recommend based on the viewer experience and any existing instructions. Explain that music should have a purpose and must leave speech intelligible. A music reference establishes taste, not permission to use that recording.

**Small result:** a short beat plan and cue list showing dialogue, ambience, music or intentional no-music. In planning practice, timing estimates remain estimates. For production, check natural delivery, pronunciation, music cues, speech masking and a viable listening/lip-sync review route.

## Step 3 — Choose the look and prepare references

**Teach:** a style image shows appearance; a character sheet records identity; an environment reference records space. One does not prove the others.

**Guide:** offer a relevant style choice only if missing, then produce or specify one representative frame. Reuse accepted identity and wardrobe. For character sheets, follow the skill's front, both profiles, back and relevant three-quarter/head/detail coverage; keep clean individual model inputs separate from the human review board. Verify product shape/operation and layout sources rather than inventing facts.

**Small result:** a reference pack or, in planning practice, explicit reference specifications and prompts. Review facial consistency, costume asymmetry, grip/contact, readable signs, materials and night/day exposure. Label generated unseen angles as interpretations. Avoid requiring a character sheet for a landscape montage without a recurring person.

## Step 4 — Select tools and preview movement

**Teach:** choose software for the problem it solves. A roadmap listing an application does not require installing or using it.

Use [tool routing](tool-routing.md) and load only the chosen adapters. Offer a recommended route plus at most two useful alternatives with effort/control tradeoffs. Record availability before promising execution.

| Component | When and why | How to introduce it in the lesson | When to skip |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blender | Stable 3D layout, repeated angles, exact objects, controlled camera movement | Start with a simple scene/blockout, set a camera and timing, preview movement, then improve materials/light only after it works | Good existing footage, loose one-off look exploration, or editing/sound alone |
| DaVinci Resolve | Assemble clips, test pacing, mix dialogue/music/ambience, match color and export | Import a few clips, choose timeline settings, make a rough cut, add sound, review a short export | A still-only task or a suitable existing editor already handles the work |
| After Effects | Detailed animated typography, layered graphics, reusable Adobe templates or a specific compositing need | Make one short composition, animate a few editable elements, preview timing, export a tested intermediate for the edit | Basic cuts, a simple title or a task Resolve/Fusion already handles well |
| Image/video generation | Explore looks or create supported shots/components | Build a prompt from accepted decisions, bind the actual supported references and evaluate a bounded result | Exact motion/text/geometry cannot be preserved by the available model; use controlled footage/assets instead |
| Existing real footage | Authentic locations, camera motion and practical human performance | Select coverage, check continuity and build the edit around usable shots | Missing coverage or a brief requiring a different controlled fictional layout |

### Blender mini-lesson, only if selected

1. Inspect the existing scene and save a working version. Define scale and the visible space; if there is no scene, start with simple shapes to test composition.
2. Place the camera where the audience's recording lens belongs. Set framing and only the needed start/turn/end states. Preview the whole move cheaply and check clipping, reverse views, timing and parallax.
3. After motion works, improve visible assets/materials/light and test a few representative frames. A speaking realistic person needs a feasible performance/rig/compositing route too.
4. Render an appropriate sequence and test its color, frame rate and alpha, if used, in the editor. Explain what reaches the next tool: one still does not transfer 3D geometry or animation. See [Blender](tools-blender.md).

### Resolve mini-lesson, only if selected

1. Inspect clips and verify import works. Set the project timeline's aspect and frame rate deliberately, then arrange a few clips or preview frames into a rough cut.
2. Put dialogue, ambience and music on separate tracks where useful. Time words and pauses; lower music under speech as needed and listen to the combined result.
3. Match exposure/color, add only useful titles/captions and inspect continuity. Major geometry skips need source repair; grading does not fix them.
4. Export a short sample, reopen it and check motion, sound and specs before a full delivery. Verify the installed edition; do not teach a Studio-only tool as part of Free. See [Resolve](tools-resolve.md).

### After Effects mini-lesson, only if selected

1. Confirm the learner has access and name the graphic/composite that warrants it. Match composition dimensions, frame rate and duration to the edit.
2. Build one style frame using editable text/shapes or supplied layers. Animate a simple entrance, readable hold and exit; explain keyframes as saved values at different times.
3. Preview timing and check fonts, safe areas, missing sources, effects and any tracking. Do not promise a flat plate can repair arbitrary changing geometry.
4. Export a tested compatible clip/image sequence, with transparency only when needed, and check it in the editor. Preserve the editable AE source separately. See [After Effects](tools-aftereffects.md).

For hands-on software teaching, inspect or establish the installed version and visible state before giving specific controls. Give one manageable operation, expected visible result and a way to recover from the actual error. Use available authorized automation if the user wants the assistant to do it; distinguish what the assistant completed from instructions the learner still needs to perform. Do not claim to see their application or invent button names. If the user explicitly wants to learn an otherwise unnecessary tool, offer a small labeled learning exercise instead of pretending the production depends on it.

**Small result:** a storyboard/timed rough preview and a chosen production route with known limitations. Apply [production readiness](production-readiness.md) before generating accuracy-sensitive footage.

## Step 5 — Make the selected shots

**Teach:** the project brief becomes asset prompts, shot prompts, actual tool inputs/settings and finishing instructions. A long generic prompt does not replace references or a supported control.

**Guide:** write prompts from the accepted camera/character/location/action/audio decisions using [shots and prompts](shots-prompts.md). Explain the few clauses that control this shot. Bind references through the tool's actual input fields. In practice mode, stop at the usable prompt/settings plan unless real execution is requested.

For execution, verify readiness and supported settings, inspect existing job/retry state, get a fresh quote where needed and use existing sufficient authorization. If paid permission is missing, prepare the exact proposed run first. Review one meaningful result before broad production; no unbounded retries or spending for the sake of a lesson.

**Small result:** actual media plus a status record, or a clearly labeled unexecuted handoff. Look for failures across the whole clip, not just its opening frame. If a trial fails, explain the observation and choose a targeted repair, not an automatic rerun.

## Step 6 — Finish, review and learn from the result

**Guide:** use the selected editor to assemble, trim, mix sound, match color, add requested captions and export. Load [edit and motion](edit-motion.md), [audio](audio-captions.md) and [delivery](delivery.md) only for the relevant work.

Review the whole video with sound, then inspect difficult frames/transitions. Check viewpoint, character/product identity, contact/gait, stable geography/signs, dialogue pronunciation and lip-sync, music masking/cues, ambience continuity and technical specs. Screenshots/transcripts cannot certify the unheard/unwatched properties. Use a capable reviewer when necessary; mark failed and unverified criteria honestly.

**Small result:** the reviewed export and editable source if requested, or the completed practice plan. End with the files/links, what was actually made, remaining defects and a short explanation of why the chosen tools helped. Offer one optional next learning exercise instead of automatically starting another project.

## Resume note

Keep this short in the existing project record:

`tutorial or production | current step | chosen project and tools | accepted choices | completed artifacts | missing evidence | pending user choice | next action | spend already authorized/used`

On resume, state where the learner stopped and ask only the unresolved next choice. Do not carry a prior project owner's account, personal files, authorization or software access into a family member's new project.
