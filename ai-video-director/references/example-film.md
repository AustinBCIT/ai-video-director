# Worked example: a 45-second short film

Fictional planning example, not a rendered film. Proposed timing demonstrates Detailed planning around continuity while keeping assets limited.

## Brief

- Premise: a commuter notices a dropped notebook and chooses to help its owner despite being late.
- Purpose: communicate a small act of kindness through performance and sound.
- Audience/delivery: general audience, 16:9 short film, 45 seconds; 24 fps proposed.
- Tools: GPT reasoning, Higgsfield and Resolve Free; Blender allowed for layout if useful.
- Look: restrained realism, soft station-window light, quiet sound design.
- Characters: commuter CHAR-01 and owner CHAR-02; no spoken dialogue.
- Stage: preproduction package; paid generation requires an agreed envelope.

## Reference plan

Develop/accept each character independently. Each base sheet includes one canonical frontal portrait plus front, left profile, right profile and back body views with consistent head/hair and costume; add the three-quarter and close-up face angles required by the action. These are views of the same identity, not competing character designs. Submit clean separate images according to shot needs. CHAR-01 wears COST-01 throughout; CHAR-02 wears COST-02. PROP-01 is the same blue notebook in every shot.

ENV-01 records the bench, exit, window and walkway layout. Use a rough top-down diagram or blockout to establish camera positions; detailed 3D modeling is optional. Maintain the main movement from frame right toward frame left until the commuter stops. The notebook remains near the bench and is picked up with the right hand.

## Proposed coverage

| Shot | Time | Framing/action | Continuity / sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| S01 | 0-6 s | Wide: commuter heads toward exit | Establish geography; station ambience |
| S02 | 6-11 s | Insert: watch glance, moving feet | Same sleeve/watch; footsteps |
| S03 | 11-17 s | Medium: owner drops notebook beside bench | Clear cause and prop landing; small impact |
| S04 | 17-24 s | Close reaction: commuter sees it, looks toward exit | Same gaze targets/light; distant departure cue |
| S05 | 24-31 s | Medium: commuter turns and kneels | Motivation clear; right-hand approach |
| S06 | 31-38 s | Insert/two-person framing: notebook returned | Preserve notebook orientation and contact |
| S07 | 38-45 s | Quiet two-shot: acknowledgment, commuter leaves | Emotional payoff; ambience continues |

Total 45 seconds / 1080 frames at 24 fps. Let the animatic change these timings if reactions need more space. No arbitrary action-speed increase to fit a fixed table.

## Highest-risk pilot

S06 is the hardest continuity/contact shot. Test it using the accepted references before commissioning every shot. If reliable hand-to-hand contact is impractical, propose a motivated edit: the commuter places the notebook on the bench, followed by the owner retrieving it. That preserves the helpful action but changes staging; record the choice rather than silently presenting it as the original handoff.

## Shot-prompt emphasis

For S04, specify the commuter's look down toward PROP-01, brief look toward the exit, then a settled decision; keep the camera still. Avoid adding an orbit/rack focus while the performance is already demanding. Use the relevant character/environment references and recorded light direction. Do not include the second character's face reference unnecessarily.

## Audio, captions and finish

Sound provides place, urgency and choice. Use footsteps, room ambience, notebook impact and a distant departure sound at controlled levels. Music is optional. No dialogue subtitles are necessary, but accessibility captions may include meaningful non-speech cues if requested for the delivery. Do not equate no dialogue with no sound-design work.

Review adjoining shots for screen direction, right-hand use, costume, prop position, identity and emotional progression. Lower-resolution drafts can test timing but may not establish facial/contact fidelity. Upscale only accepted motion and inspect for altered detail. Finish the sound and matched color before final export. Report actual accepted shots and unresolved issues, never "film complete" from a storyboard alone.
