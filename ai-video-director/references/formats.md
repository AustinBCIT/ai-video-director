# Format-specific routes

Use only the selected row and related modules. Format does not prescribe a single style or guarantee audience response.

| Format | Primary planning question | Useful artifacts | Common mistake to prevent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short / Reel / vertical video | What does the viewer understand immediately, and what rewards finishing? | Opening beat, concise shot sequence, phone-readable graphics, ending/loop if wanted | Filling every second with cuts or captions regardless of comprehension |
| Short drama / episodic story | Who wants what, what blocks them, what changes? | Character/voice continuity, scene objectives, coverage, episode canon | Treating every shot as an unrelated attractive image |
| Viral-style experiment | What is the testable hook or shareable moment for this audience? | A few hook variants, clear payoff, dated references, measurement plan | Promising virality or copying another creator's entire execution |
| Short film | What emotional change is carried by action, image and sound? | Treatment, script, geography, blocking, storyboard/animatic where useful, sound design | Expensive coverage with no story purpose |
| Motion graphics / design | What information should the viewer notice and in what order? | Style frames, typography grid, motion timing, editable layers, visual hierarchy | Rendering exact text or charts as unstable generative video |
| Ad / product promotion | What audience problem, supportable benefit and next action matter? | Product truth/reference, offer/copy, proof or demonstration, CTA, variants | Invented performance claims or drifting product labels/geometry |
| Educational content | What should the viewer understand or be able to do afterward? | Learning objective, verified explanation, worked example, clear diagrams, recap | Visual metaphors presented as literal mechanism or unverified generated labels |
| YouTube long-form | What promise does the opening make, and how does each section repay it? | Outline/script, chapter beats, A/B-roll plan, recurring graphics, thumbnail/title | Overlong preamble, repetitive narration, stock-like visuals unrelated to the argument |
| UGC / presenter / tutorial | Is the product the hero, or is a person demonstrating/experiencing it? | Performance script, real-use steps, product references, voice/dialogue plan | Routing every UGC request through a product-only or faceless workflow |

## Format-specific judgment

For Shorts, test the opening at actual phone size and with sound off when the viewing context warrants it. A clear opening can be quiet, funny, beautiful or confrontational; do not impose a fixed three-second gimmick. Put essential text away from current platform UI overlays, verified for the intended placement.

For drama, maintain a scene objective and emotional turn; include reaction shots and purposeful silence. Design a cliffhanger only if an episode benefits from it. For films, prioritize coverage that protects the edit: an establishing view where geography matters, the action/reaction needed for the scene, and inserts only when useful.

For ads, separate brand awareness, consideration and conversion goals. A cinematic atmosphere spot and a product demonstration require different evidence and pacing. Generate optional hook/CTA variants only within the requested count and authorized spend.

For education, set the audience's starting knowledge. Use real data and deterministic diagrams for factual relationships. Cite source-dependent claims and distinguish a simplified diagram from measured proportions. Add an accessible transcript or meaningful sound descriptions when needed.

For long-form, estimate runtime by reading/speaking the actual script and leaving space for visuals, pauses and transitions. Chapters are structural aids; they do not substitute for narrative progression. Packaging must accurately reflect the content.

## Starting with existing footage

Inspect what exists before inventing additional scenes. Identify usable action, sound, resolution, orientation and coverage gaps. Offer a plan based on the real material. Label any reconstruction, replacement, stock or generated insert that affects authenticity. Follow the chosen editing skill only after this inventory.
