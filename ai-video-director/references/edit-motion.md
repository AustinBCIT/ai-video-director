# Editing, motion graphics and compositing

## Assembly before polish

Inventory usable clips, takes, graphics and sound. Preserve originals and identify missing coverage. Build the story/argument with rough sound before fine grading and effects. For generated material, trim unstable starts/ends only if enough intended action remains. Do not crop away a required product feature just to hide a defect.

Choose cuts for information, action, reaction or emotion. Establish a rhythm appropriate to comprehension; faster is not inherently better. Use J-cuts (next sound begins before its image), L-cuts (previous sound continues under the next image), match cuts or cutaways when they serve continuity. Do not add decorative transitions to every boundary.

## Motion design specification

Define a style frame, hierarchy, exact text, typography, color, alignment and safe region. Then define start state, entrance, readable hold, exit, easing and motion blur. Use seconds/frames tied to the timeline. For reusable templates, expose text/color/layout controls and plan bounds for longer copy/localization.

Example: at 30 fps a title enters over 8 frames, remains readable for 60 frames and exits over 6 frames. These values are a starting design choice; time the real text and context. A long sentence will need more reading time. Keep logos and data visualization geometry controlled rather than regenerated across frames.

## Compositing handoff

When combining Blender, generated plates and graphics, specify framing, resolution, frame rate, frame range, camera match, color interpretation, alpha mode and needed passes. Use clean plates, masks, tracked points, shadows, reflections or depth passes only when they solve the actual composite. Match perspective, light direction, edges, grain/noise and motion blur before stylistic polish.

Avoid treating a flattened generative image as editable geometry. Use 2.5D layers for limited parallax when they suffice; reveal of unseen surfaces may require 3D or new imagery. Disclose that limitation in the plan.

## Choose an editor for a reason

Resolve Free is the default candidate for assembly, sound, color and modest Fusion work. After Effects can be useful for extensive typography, custom animation, tracking/compositing or an existing Adobe template workflow. Blender is useful for 3D scenes/cameras and procedural motion. Use the user's allowed tools; do not insist on a new application when the selected one can meet the brief.

Avoid unnecessary round trips. For AE/Resolve interchange, render a short test in a mutually supported format before producing every shot; do not assume an editable timeline or effect graph transfers intact. A rendered handoff can be correct when editability is not required. Save the editable source separately when requested.

## Color

Determine source color interpretation before applying a look. Balance mismatched shots, protect skin/product colors and choose an output transform appropriate to the destination. Generated video is not automatically camera log or scene-linear HDR. Do not apply a camera LUT merely because the prompt named that camera. Check for clipped highlights, banding, excessive sharpening and unstable grain.

## Review in context

Verify structure, continuity, timing, spelling, full-motion artifacts, sound, captions and platform crops. Review the exported file as well as the timeline. If a review reveals a defect, use the [director repair decision](repair-cards.md#director-repair-decision) to compare repair, re-edit and regeneration before choosing the smallest adequate fix. Freeze a working version before a substantial experiment so revisions remain reversible.
