# Purpose, audience, research and trends

## Define the outcome

Gather the message/agenda, audience knowledge, desired feeling/action, placement and success signal. "Beautiful" is a style goal; "make the product's folding mechanism understandable" is an observable communication goal. Preserve both when supplied.

For marketing, include positioning, offer, proof, objections, CTA and required brand copy only when relevant. For entertainment, use premise, emotion and payoff rather than forcing a sales funnel. For education, use learning outcome and comprehension. Choose metrics that match the goal: qualified response, comprehension, completion, saves or conversion; do not optimize only views by default.

## Trend mode

Offer `off`, `light` or `research` when the project could benefit. Default to light only if discovery value is clear; a timeless film or precise tutorial may need none. Research when the user asks what is current, or when a recommendation depends on present popularity or tool availability.

1. Narrow platform, topic, audience, language and region. Ask only missing distinctions that would alter results.
2. Inspect a small useful set of recent primary examples or platform trend tools. Record URLs, publication dates, date checked and any visible performance data. Use an appropriate time window; fast audio trends may need days, genre patterns may need months.
3. Separate observation from inference. Views alone do not establish causality or prove a trend; account size, advertising, distribution and posting age can explain differences.
4. Extract transferable choices: opening information, camera viewpoint, reveal structure, rhythm, visual motif, participation mechanic or sound use. Propose an original application consistent with the user's goal.
5. State evidence limits. If only older or inaccessible examples are available, label the direction unverified; do not call it trending now.

Output a short card: `pattern | source/date | what is observed | why it might fit | change to test | risk/tradeoff`. Usually two or three relevant directions are enough. User can choose evergreen instead.

## Comparisons and tests

When helpful, keep the core piece stable and vary one meaningful factor, such as opening image or framing of the promise. Define the audience, placement, outcome and time window before claiming a winner. A tiny informal test is directional evidence, not statistical proof. Do not fabricate performance projections or benchmark percentages.

## Rights and claims at the relevant stage

Track licenses for actual music, footage, fonts and stock assets selected for release. Check commercial-use terms for the generation route actually used. Flag a concrete missing right or unsupported factual claim where it affects production/release; do not begin every creative interview with a generic compliance checklist. Never create fake testimonials or product capabilities. If a visual is illustrative, do not describe it as evidence of a real result.

## Example

Brief: a 20-second folding-desk ad for people in small apartments. Purpose: show that it can free floor space. Useful opening test: start with the mechanism already moving versus begin on the cramped room. The user needs actual product dimensions and a truthful demonstration; a fashionable lens or trending song will not fix an inaccurate hinge.
